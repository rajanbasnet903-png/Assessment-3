Q4 – NOTEBOOKLM EXPERIMENT RESULTS

Experiment:

Using NotebookLM to analyse sustainability documents for an academic ESG briefing.

Sources:

Microsoft 2024 Environmental Sustainability Report
Unilever Sustainability Statement


EXPERIMENT 1 – SOURCE INGESTION

Both sustainability documents were added to the same NotebookLM notebook.

Result:

NotebookLM successfully organised the documents as sources within one workspace. This provided a single environment for asking questions and comparing information from both organisations.

Usefulness:

This feature was useful because students or researchers can analyse several long documents without constantly switching between separate PDF files.


EXPERIMENT 2 – ESG SUMMARY

Task:

NotebookLM was asked to summarise the main sustainability priorities under environmental, social and governance themes.

Result:

The generated response provided a structured overview of the main sustainability topics discussed across the uploaded sources.

The response was easier to review than reading both complete sustainability documents separately and provided source references that supported verification.


EXPERIMENT 3 – THEMATIC ANALYSIS

Task:

NotebookLM was asked to identify five important sustainability themes across the two documents.

Result:

The tool identified recurring environmental and sustainability themes and linked them to the relevant organisations.

Themes included areas such as climate action, environmental resource management, waste or circularity and organisational sustainability commitments.

Usefulness:

Thematic analysis was useful for quickly identifying common areas of ESG concern across large reports.


EXPERIMENT 4 – MICROSOFT AND UNILEVER COMPARISON

Task:

NotebookLM was asked to identify similarities and differences between Microsoft and Unilever's sustainability approaches.

Result:

The tool generated a structured comparison that distinguished shared ESG priorities from organisation-specific approaches.

The comparison demonstrated that NotebookLM can synthesise information across more than one source rather than simply summarising each document separately.


EXPERIMENT 5 – ACADEMIC BRIEFING

Task:

NotebookLM was asked to prepare a short evidence-based academic briefing.

Result:

The output organised information into a concise format that could support university study, research preparation or professional briefing activities.

Usefulness:

This reduced the time required to convert long sustainability documents into a readable academic overview.


EXPERIMENT 6 – SOURCE-GROUNDED QUESTION ANSWERING

Task:

NotebookLM was asked about Microsoft and Unilever's climate and carbon commitments.

Result:

The generated response remained connected to the uploaded documents and provided source references that could be checked against the reports.

Usefulness:

Citation support increased transparency because users could return to the source material rather than relying only on the AI-generated answer.


OVERALL RESULT

NotebookLM performed well as a supporting research tool for summarisation, thematic analysis, comparison and source-grounded question answering.

Its strongest advantage was the ability to work from a controlled collection of uploaded sources.

However, the generated information should not automatically be treated as fully accurate. Important targets, definitions and contextual details should still be checked against the original documents.
